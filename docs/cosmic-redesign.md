# Cosmic presentation

A dark mineral palette, pale ceramic instruments, a custom ascent mark, and vector atlas
plates with a light arcade influence. The shell, the ten player screens, navigation and
discipline icons, destination details, equipment art, collection, hangar and recap all
share it. Rank names, colours and gameplay facts keep their existing meaning.

## Star chart and travel

The six destinations are real Three.js geometry: procedural surfaces, orbital structures,
crystals and moons. One renderer moves between the chart and the hangar, and the equipped
ship changes the model. Pointer movement gives the chart a little parallax; the native
destination buttons stay the primary keyboard controls.

Routine travel is 900 ms. A first arrival is 3200 ms and offers Skip flight; Escape also
ends it. First arrivals are remembered locally under `apogee.cosmic.arrivals`. A major
clear gets a dismissible reveal of at most 6500 ms. All of it is runtime animation, not
video, and the visual layer stores no campaign progress.

Rendering is capped at 35 fps and a pixel ratio of 1.5. It stops off-screen, on blur,
when paused and under reduced motion. The chart has zoom, reset and objective controls.
If WebGL fails, the flat destination map and list remain. Reduced motion selects a
destination immediately. Closing a dialog or ending travel puts keyboard focus back.

## Atlas plates

The screen headers use eight SVG plates: six destination symbols, the final passage and a
ship. Muted phosphor colours, wireframe geometry and small registration marks, with the
texture kept inside the illustration so text stays clean. They are generated by
`node tools/buildAtlas.mjs`; rerun it after changing the plate source. The standalone
preview embeds the SVGs.

## Building and checking

- `npm.cmd start` runs the desktop client. Quit any running copy first: its
  single-instance lock keeps the old build alive.
- `npm.cmd run build:cosmic` rebuilds `cosmic.js` from `cosmic-source.mjs`. App builds,
  preview builds and `validate:expedition-ui` run it themselves.
- `tools/apogee-ui-preview.html` is the standalone preview: real snapshot data and labelled
  sample multiplayer content. Launching the game and saving campaign progress need the
  desktop app.

`validate:expedition-ui` drives ten screens at 1440×1080, 940×640 and 600×900 outer window
sizes (the usable area is smaller once the frame is subtracted). It covers the four
journeys, all 122 rewards, equipment, compact layouts, keyboard controls and reduced
motion. The cosmic cases render WebGL, compare arrival and revisit timing, skip flights,
pause frames, reuse the canvas and geometry, suspend off-screen rendering, simulate
context loss and restoration, and check focus after a reveal. Foreground signals are
simulated in the hidden fixture. Screenshots land in `.cache/expedition-ui`.

The campaign validators cover v2 migration and v3 persistence, and the desktop smoke test
boots against real local stats. The sound check measures clipping and the relative level
of all 13 cues; it says nothing about whether they sound good. Matchmaking, Steam sign-in,
online settlement and a real KovaaK's session are outside all of these.
